Abstract

Graphite felt (GF), a porous carbon electrode, has few active sites and intrinsic hydrophobicity, which limits its electrochemical performance. Various modification methods—such as surface treatments, coatings with carbon nanomaterials, and metal oxide deposition—have been investigated to overcome these issues1. Still, problems such as poor long-term stability, nanoparticle agglomeration, and weak bonding between the electrode and modifiers persist. Graphitic carbon nitride (g-C3N4), a metal-free 2D material known for its chemical stability, high nitrogen content, and strong hydrophilicity, offers a promising alternative for electrode enhancement2. In this study, different graphitic carbon nitride (g-C3N4) nanostructures were uniformly decorated on GF via thermal polymerization to improve its redox kinetics for vanadium flow batteries (VRFBs). The nano g-C3N4/GF electrode showed better hydrophilicity, lower charge-transfer resistance, and superior electrochemical activity. Optimal g-C3N4 distribution minimized agglomeration and increased active sites for VO²⁺/VO₂⁺ redox reactions. Improved charge transport and electrolyte access lowered polarization and raised energy efficiency to 84.13% at 80 mA/cm².The enhancement is attributed to strong π–π interactions between g-C3N4 and GF, which increased adhesion, surface area, and electrolyte accessibility. It also had excellent cycling stability over 1000 cycles, proving durability. These findings highlight g-C3N4 as a low-cost, high-performance alternative to noble metal VRFB electrodes.
